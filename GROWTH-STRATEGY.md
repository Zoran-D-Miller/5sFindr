# 5sFindr: Growth, Competition and Brand Strategy (draft)

> Drafted 2026-11-13. This is a plan only. Nothing has been built or changed yet.
> Items marked **DECIDE** need Zoran's call before they go into `PRODUCT-BRIEF.md`.

**Decided 2026-11-14:**
- **Positioning:** organiser tool + social layer. Paid courts are the norm, so the money flow on paid
  courts has to work well (section 3a).
- **Launch:** soft-launch to organisers now in 1–2 areas, then open each area to players once it reaches a
  player threshold. This replaces the "15 organisers + 300 players" gate.
- **Brand:** blend crafted and underground. See section 6a ("Open door, inner circle").
- **Online only for now:** Zoran can't travel. Anything that needs someone in person goes through
  partner organisers ("Area Captains"). Section 7a is the online-only plan.
- **Paid courts:** a "who's paid" tracker now, in-app share payment later. Every flow has to be as
  low-friction as possible, because that's what makes it stick (section 3b).
- **Areas:** City Bowl + Atlantic Seaboard, Southern Suburbs, Northern Suburbs. Organisers can
  join from any of them straight away (the tool works for their own group). Player campaigns push **one
  area at a time**, whichever is closest to unlocking, so free reach isn't spread over three areas.
- **Budget: R0 for now.** Free channels only. The Claude subscription covers the product videos
  and carousels. Paid items in section 7a are parked until there's budget.

## Summary

1. **Cape Town already has competitors.** Pitchd (court booking plus paid pickup games, already selling
   Fives Futbol slots, including Maynard Mall), FootyFinder (R80 refereed games where you pick your
   position, live since about Oct 2026) and Cape Town Play5 (social games for travellers and expats, run
   through Meetup) are all running. 5sFindr has 3 waitlist rows, all of them our own test signups.
2. **Don't compete on paid court booking.** Every competitor charges R75–R150 a game and either runs the
   game itself or sells the court. The space nobody holds: a **free tool for people who already organise
   games** (the WhatsApp group admins), **free open-field games**, and a **real social layer** (meeting
   people, regulars, newcomers). That last one is our stated reason to exist, but the product doesn't
   show it yet.
3. **What the video means for us:** people convert when they *use* a product, not when they *watch* it.
   Our site is a form with three text cards. Before asking for a signup, show real games and a short
   tap-through demo, with the "oh, I get it" moment on the first screen.
4. **Premium look:** yes to *crafted and culture-led*, no to *luxury or exclusive*. A newcomer has to
   feel welcome, and an organiser has to feel safe sending the link to their group.
5. **Underground brand:** don't build a secret brand. Do use honest scarcity on top of an open product:
   areas that unlock as players join, numbered founding members, and a monthly game where the pitch
   is only revealed the day before. Be clear about *what* the product is and mysterious about *where*
   and *who*.

### Notes on each

**Pitchd (pitchd.co.za)**: biggest direct threat.
- Strengths: already signed the **Fives Futbol chain**, including Maynard Mall, which `PRODUCT-BRIEF.md`
  names as one of our launch venue partners. Live court times, split payments, refund if a game is
  cancelled, skill filters, SEO pages per area ("5-a-side Southern Suburbs"), runs paid ads, and has a
  "list your venue free" pitch for venue owners.
- Weaknesses: it's a booking tool. Only **2 open games** were listed, so they have courts but few
  players. You pay before you play, there's nothing social, and it does nothing for free park games
  or for groups that already have a regular slot.
- Their "Short on players? Don't cancel. Open it up" reel uses **our organiser pitch**, but only works
  on their courts and with payment up front.

**FootyFinder (footyfinder.co.za)**: closest in name and audience. Registered in 2026, based in Woodstock.
- Strengths: you **pick your exact position on a pitch diagram** (no more being stuck in goal), a
  referee at every game, goals and assists on your profile, leaderboards, a captain can pay for mates,
  credit or refund, 11-a-side (rare), and a clear "WhatsApp group vs FootyFinder" comparison table.
- Weaknesses: it runs every game itself (refs, pitch hire), so growth is limited by how many games
  its staff can run. Two venues, paid only, competitive and stats-heavy rather than social.
- **Name risk:** "5sFindr" and "FootyFinder" sound alike, and `footyfinder.app` is another unrelated
  product. People searching may mix us up.

**Cape Town Play5 (Meetup + Facebook)**: their audience *is* our audience.
- "A social football community for travellers, expats and locals," R150 a game, Mouille Point and Tekkerz.
- Strengths: they found the traveller audience by listing on Meetup, where travellers already look.
- Weaknesses: one organiser, expensive, no product. **Better as a partner than a competitor:** give
  them a free tool and they bring their players.

**Stranger Soccer (strangersoccer.com)**: the model to learn from, and a possible future entrant.
- 100,000 games, 75,000 users, 10 countries, **Durban added recently**, and new cities open through
  local partners ("Bring Stranger Soccer to your city"). It could reach Cape Town without warning.
- What they get right: a **trained Official Host at every game** (welcoming, keeps teams fair),
  first game free, memberships (vouchers, priority waiting list, free birthday game), refer and earn,
  follow players you liked playing with.

**FootyConnect (footyconnect.netlify.app)**: low threat.
- ELO skill rating and a skill assessment, plus a Premium tier. Its numbers ("1,000+ players, 8 SA
  cities, 95% satisfaction") are on a free Netlify subdomain and look aspirational.
- Idea worth taking: **skill level on games**, so beginners don't get hammered and good players don't get bored.

**Nine98 (nine98.io)**: not a competitor. Great ideas for keeping people coming back.
- "Strava for football." Log goals, assists and clean sheets, then **swap digital jerseys** with
  teammates and opponents by QR code, and share post-match cards.
- The point: it turns a game into a **record of who you played with**, which is exactly "meet people
  who play," made visible.

### What the market tells us

- Everyone else **charges per game and runs or sells the game**. That's an operations business. Each
  extra game costs them money and staff time.
- Nobody serves the **organiser who already has a group** but loses 2 players every week, unless they
  move onto a paid court (Pitchd).
- Nobody serves **free open-field games** (Green Point park, Rondebosch Common, school fields).
- Nobody makes the **social side** the product. Play5 does it in person, Nine98 in software (not in SA,
  not for finding games).
- Nobody is marketing to **women's and mixed games**, **30+ players**, or **complete beginners**.

## 3. Positioning: how we win

**Don't try to be the biggest football app in Cape Town. Be the one that turns a game into a crew.**

- **For organisers:** "Short a player? Fill it in minutes, free." Replace the WhatsApp poll with a link:
  live list, waiting list, "still coming?" reminders, show-up rates. It's useful even with **zero
  other users** (their own group uses it), which solves the empty-marketplace problem.
- **For players:** "Football is the fastest way to make friends in Cape Town." Built around people:
  who's playing, regulars, newcomers welcomed.
- **Complement, don't fight:** a game on a Pitchd-booked Fives court can still be posted on 5sFindr to
  fill it. We're the people layer, not the booking layer.

### 3a. Money on paid courts

How it works today, in real life: the organiser books and pays for the court. Players then pay their
share (court cost ÷ players) in cash at the pitch or by EFT, before or after kick-off. The organiser
chases whoever "pays later" and covers the gap when someone drops out. FootyFinder's site calls
chasing cash the #1 pain point, and that's why Pitchd and FootyFinder both take payment up front.

How the code works today (`0004_lifecycle.sql`): players buy R20 tokens from 5sFindr. Joining a game
locks one token. Cancel late and the token is **forfeited to 5sFindr** (`platform_ledger`). Show up
and you get it back. The token **doesn't cover the court share**, and when someone cancels late the
**organiser is still out of pocket** while 5sFindr keeps the R20. Organisers could reasonably see that
as a cash grab.

Options (**DECIDE**, asked in chat):
- **A. Tracker now, in-app share next.** v1: show "R__ each" up front, keep the R20 commitment
  token, and give the organiser a "who's paid" checklist (cash/EFT/SnapScan, marked by hand). No money
  passes through us. Next step: B, once organisers ask for it.
- **B. Pay your share in the app to reserve.** The player pays their court share by card/EFT when they
  join, and Paystack split payments send it to the organiser's bank account. No more chasing cash, and
  it's the revenue path from the brief (a small fee). Costs: each organiser connects a bank account
  (Paystack subaccount), plus refund and cancellation rules and payout support.
- **C. Keep the R20 token as planned**, with no tracker.

Whichever we pick: if a deposit is forfeited, **it should go to the organiser** (or count against
their court cost), not to 5sFindr. That's fairer and easier to sell to organisers.

**Decided: A.** The "who's paid" tracker comes first. Paying your share in the app comes later.

### 3b. Low friction everywhere (this is what makes it stick)

Rule: **each extra tap or form field loses people.** Competitors make you pay before you play. We win
if joining and organising a game takes less effort than the WhatsApp group does.

**Players**
- **Join from the WhatsApp link in 2 taps.** Today, auth is email + password only. That's the biggest
  friction point. Options: sign in with Google, a magic link, or join with just name + number and
  create the account afterwards.
- **One button: "I'm in."** Choosing a position or adding notes is optional and comes after.
- **Pay the organiser directly from the game page:** show the organiser's SnapScan/EFT details with
  an auto-made reference ("5S-THU-ZORAN"). One tap to copy, one tap to say "I've paid." No money passes through us.
- **Ask to install the app after the first game, not before.**

**Organisers**
- **Post a game in under 30 seconds:** saved venues, last game's settings filled in, and "repeat weekly" on by default.
- **A WhatsApp-native share:** one tap copies a message laid out like the lists they already use
  ("Thu 19:00 · Fives Maynard · R60 each · 1. Sipho 2. Zoran … 3 spots left → link").
- **A "who's paid" checklist:** tap a name to mark it paid. Players who tapped "I've paid" are shown first.
- **Spots fill themselves:** the waiting list moves up automatically, and a "still coming?" check runs at 24h/3h (S4).

**What makes them come back**
- A recurring game means the organiser never re-posts and players always know when the next one is.
- Paid status and show-up rates are always visible, so the organiser stops chasing.
- "Played with" + regulars: the network grows after every game, which makes switching to another app costly.

Tagline options (pick one later):
- "Find your game. Find your people."
- "Come alone. Leave with a crew."
- "Cape Town's 5-a-side people."

## 5. Should the design be more premium?

**Yes, but premium should mean *crafted*, not *exclusive*.** It matters more than it seems, for three reasons:
- **Trust:** you're asking people to turn up and play with strangers. A polished, real-looking brand
  makes that feel safe. A plain dark template makes it feel risky.
- **Organisers judge the link:** they won't send something that looks amateur to a group of 15 mates.
- **Shareability:** good-looking game cards and player cards get reposted. That's free reach.

What "crafted" means here:
- **Real people and real Cape Town:** photos of actual games, Table Mountain light, the Sea Point
  promenade, Grand Central at night. Use free stock until we have our own (see PRODUCT-BRIEF's
  copyright note).
- **Show the product:** game cards, faces, spots left. Not three feature boxes.
- **A football-culture look**, closer to Nike Run Club or Strava than a booking app. Strong type, motion, kit-inspired detail.
- **Social proof once it's real:** "34 players joined in Sea Point this week," organiser quotes.

What to avoid: black-and-gold luxury, velvet-rope language, "members only." That fights "free" and
"come alone," and it scares off exactly the newcomers we want.

Order: settle positioning (section 3) first, then redesign the landing page with the demo, then the app.

## 6. The "underground / secret" idea: verdict

**The psychology that makes it tempting:** curiosity gaps (people want to close an open loop),
scarcity (we want what's limited), and belonging (being an insider feels good). These work. Gmail
invites, Robinhood's waitlist, Sofar Sounds' secret-location gigs and run clubs all used them.

**Why a fully secret brand would hurt 5sFindr:**
1. **We need numbers to work.** A game-finder is useless without enough players in one area. Gating
   people out works against the one thing we most need.
2. **It scares the target user.** Newcomers and travellers already worry about not fitting in.
   "Exclusive" makes that worse. Our promise is "come alone, you're welcome."
3. **Mystery needs proof behind it.** Clubhouse had celebrities, Gmail was clearly better. With 0
   followers and 0 games, a cryptic page reads as empty or a scam, especially when it involves meeting strangers.
4. **Competitors are clear.** Someone who Googles "5-a-side Cape Town" and lands on a riddle will pick
   Pitchd or FootyFinder.

**What to do instead: be open to everyone and hard to forget.** These are honest scarcity mechanics:
- **Unlock your area.** Each neighbourhood opens when it hits a player threshold, with a progress bar:
  "Sea Point: 38/50 players. Invite 2 mates to unlock it sooner." This creates curiosity, gives people a
  reason to refer, **and** solves our density problem, all at once. **Strongest idea in this doc.**
- **Numbered founding members:** "You're Founding Member #047." The first 500 get the year-1 Premium
  perk. The scarcity is real, so it's honest.
- **Secret Sessions** (borrowed from Sofar Sounds): one free 5sFindr social game a month. Spots are
  limited and the pitch is revealed 24h before kick-off to the people who claimed one. It makes good
  content and gives the brand something to talk about. **Someone has to be there to run it** (see questions).
- **Insider culture, not insider access:** a name for regulars, a post-game photo ritual, merch later.

Don't: lock the core product behind invites, hide what the product is, or fake scarcity
("only 3 spots left!" when there are 20).

### 6a. The blend: "Open door, inner circle"

**Underground in how it looks and in its rituals. Open in who can get in.** No one is ever blocked
from joining a game, because that's the north star (spots filled by strangers). We gate only the
*extras*, and people **earn** them by doing the things that grow the north star.

**Layer 1: The look (what everyone sees)**
- Night football under floodlights, flash photography, film grain, raw condensed type, stencil kit numbers.
- Mostly monochrome, with pitch green kept as the one "signal" colour.
- Coordinates and times as graphic elements ("33.91° S, 18.41° E · THU 19:00").
- References: Corteiz's secret-location drops, cage/5s football culture in London, Sofar Sounds.
- All of this is visual, so it costs nothing extra to make online.

**Layer 2: The message (always plain)**
- **Two-line rule:** every teaser has a plain line underneath it.
  "Sea Point unlocks Friday." / "Free 5-a-side games near you. Join the list to get in first."
- Link previews, search results and the WhatsApp invite page always say plainly what the product is
  and that it's free.

**Layer 3: The inner circle (earned, never bought, no invite needed)**
| Mechanic | How it works | How you earn it | North star link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founding Member # | "Member #047", shown on your profile and player card | Join before your area unlocks | Gets people in early |
| Unlock your area | A public progress bar for each neighbourhood | Invite mates | Builds density |
| The Drop | New games in an area are released at a set time each week (e.g. Wed 19:00) | Turn on alerts | An online appointment, so people come back |
| Sessions | Monthly game at a secret location, revealed 24h before, run by an Area Captain | Show up 3 times, or bring a new player who shows up | Rewards showing up and bringing strangers |
| Captain status | Badge, featured games, founding Premium | Run a weekly game | Supply |

Online-only fit: Sessions are run by **Area Captains** (partner organisers), not Zoran. 5sFindr
provides the drop, the location reveal, a content template and the perks. The first Sessions can
simply be an organiser's regular game with 4 spots opened to the list.

## 7. Growth and stickiness: what to do, in order

### Getting players and organisers
1. **Organisers first, where they already are:** Cape Town 5-a-side FB groups (FootyFinder and Play5
   both post there), r/capetown, Meetup, office and varsity groups. Pitch: "Your group, your slot,
   free. Never cancel a game for being 2 short." Target: **15 organisers who each run one weekly game**.
2. **Travellers and newcomers, where they already are:** a free **Meetup group** (that's how Play5 found
   them), hostels and co-working spaces on the Atlantic Seaboard and City Bowl (they often have events
   boards and WhatsApp groups), expat and nomad groups, and international student offices at UCT and UWC.
3. **Every game link is an ad:** make the `/m/<slug>` WhatsApp preview show spots left plus players'
   faces. Joining from a link should be as quick as possible.
4. **Unlock-your-area referrals** (section 6).
5. **Shareable cards:** an auto-generated "game recap" for the organiser ("10 players, 4 new faces,
   Green Point, Thursday") and a "player card" for IG stories, borrowed from Nine98.

### Keeping them (stickiness)
1. **Recurring games:** organisers post once and it repeats weekly. This is what organisers need most,
   and it builds a weekly habit for players.
2. **No-show tools** (already S4): "still coming?" reminders, spots released automatically, show-up rate.
3. **"Played with":** after each game, add the people you played with. Get told when your regulars
   join a game. (This is Nine98's jersey swap, adapted for finding games.)
4. **A welcome for newcomers:** a "first game in Cape Town" tag on the roster, so the organiser (or
   "Captain," already in the code) knows to say hi. (This is Stranger Soccer's host idea without paid staff.)
5. **Game labels:** skill level (beginner / casual / competitive), and **women's, mixed and 30+ games**.
   No local competitor serves these.

### 7a. Making a splash online only (no travel)
1. **Organiser recruitment by DM and posts:** Cape Town 5-a-side FB groups, r/capetown, Meetup
   organisers (including Play5), IG football pages, varsity club pages, and company social clubs on
   LinkedIn. One script, one "founding organiser" offer (founding Premium, Captain badge, featured games).
2. **Area Captains:** the first serious organiser in each area becomes its Captain. They're the face
   in person. We support them online.
3. **The Unlock as the launch moment:** a public area board on the site, a countdown, an IG story
   each time an area hits a milestone, and founding numbers called out.
4. **A faceless content engine** (Remotion + stock footage + screen recordings + AI voiceover +
   captions), 3–4 posts a week. Formats:
   - "POV: you just moved to Cape Town and don't know anyone"
   - "Short 2 players at 17:00: watch the game fill" (a screen recording)
   - "Every WhatsApp football group has these 5 people"
   - A Cape Town 5s venue tier list
   - Area unlock countdowns
   - Organiser spotlights (they send phone clips, we edit them)
5. **Borrow audiences (free):** swap value with Cape Town football, student and lifestyle pages
   (we feature their games, they share our area unlocks); expat and nomad Facebook/WhatsApp groups;
   hostels and co-working spaces by email, with a printable QR poster PDF they put up. Note: *running*
   a Meetup group costs money, so for now ask organisers who are already on Meetup (like Play5) to link
   their 5sFindr game page.
6. **Carousels** (Zoran makes them): "WhatsApp group vs 5sFindr" (FootyFinder's best section, in our
   own words), "5 types of player in every 5s group," area unlock progress, Founding Member numbers.
7. **Free production stack:** Remotion (free for individuals and small teams), Pexels/Pixabay
   footage, a free AI voice tier (check the attribution rules) or Zoran's own voice, CapCut auto-captions.
8. *Parked until there's budget:* a Meta ads test (Cape Town, 18–40, football interest → area
   unlock page), paid creator shoutouts, a paid voice plan.

### Measuring it
- Keep the north star from the brief: **spots filled by strangers per week**.
- Add **organiser week-2 retention** (did they post again?) and **waitlist → first game** conversion.

## 8. Things you didn't ask about but should know

1. **Two competitors weren't on your list:** FootyFinder and Cape Town Play5. Both are live in Cape
   Town, and Play5 targets exactly our audience.
2. **Our venue plan is weakened.** Pitchd already sells Fives Futbol slots, including Maynard Mall.
   Going after the same chain puts us second. Better: **open fields, schools, clubs, churches, office
   parks**, and Tekkerz-style independents, where we bring the *players*, not the booking system.
3. **The launch gate may be too slow now.** "15 organisers + 300 players before launch" made sense
   when the market was empty. With three live competitors and 0 signups after 3 weeks, waiting for
   a fixed bar risks being late. Alternative: **soft-launch the organiser tool now in 1–2 areas**
   (it works even with no other users), then open each area as it fills. **DECIDE**
4. **Organisers can't get in yet.** The gate redirects `/signup`. This blocks item 3 and the early-access plan.
5. **Name confusion with FootyFinder.** Not urgent, but the brand has to look and sound clearly
   different. Also decide how "5sFindr" is said ("Fives Finder"?) and use that in every voiceover.
6. **Stranger Soccer could arrive** through a local partner at any time. Our protection is organiser
   relationships and a social graph (who played with whom). Those are hard to copy. Payments and courts are easy to copy.
7. **Women's, mixed, 30+ and beginner games** have no local champion. Choosing one could give us the
   first area/segment we own.

## 12. Using competitors' audiences (2026-11-22)

**Decided 2026-11-22:**
- **Goal:** use competitors' audiences to grow ours. Never send our players into their games.
- **Never name competitors in public:** no names, tags, logos or named comparisons. Price contrasts stay
  unnamed ("R65–R150 elsewhere, free here").
- **No pressure tactics.** Only talk partnership with small organisers (Play5-type), and only once we have
  players to offer them. Don't approach the platforms (Pitchd, FootyFinder): it only tells them our plan.
- **Order:** build the organiser lead list now. S1 essentials → S2 → S3 come before any outreach, so
  every lead lands on a game they can join, not a waitlist.
- **Win condition:** density per area, not citywide size. Be the place with the most weekly games in one
  area, then the next. Track our weekly games per area against what competitors list.

**What we saw on 2026-11-22:**
- pitchd.co.za lists **1 open game** (Tekkerz, Sat 28 Nov, R85). On 14 Nov it listed 2. Their "spots left"
  posts are them trying to fill games, not spare players we could catch.
- Pitchd's IG copy now leans social: "See who's in before you commit", "familiar faces and some new ones".
  Our messaging lead is shrinking. Free games, open fields, the organiser tool and "played with" still hold.
- Stranger Soccer is openly recruiting city "License Owners". A Cape Town licensee could appear without warning.

**Playbook:**
1. **Organiser lead list (start now, contact after S3):** people opening short games on Pitchd, small
   organisers, FB group admins. One personal message each: "Keep your booking where it is, post the game
   here too, free. Here's the WhatsApp list it makes."
2. **Gap list:** read comments under competitor posts (never reply there). Log asks nobody serves:
   price, beginners, women's/mixed, 30+, areas with no games.
3. **Unnamed price-contrast content.**
4. **SEO page:** "Pickup football in Cape Town for travellers and newcomers" (catches people who used
   Stranger Soccer abroad).
5. **Weekly roundup of independent games only** (our organisers + WhatsApp-group games, never platform games).
6. **Game page line for courts booked elsewhere:** "Court booked by the organiser, pay them directly."

**Don'ts:** scraping listings or followers; mass DMs (POPIA s69 needs consent for unsolicited electronic
direct marketing); replies under their posts; fake accounts; their names in handles, hashtags or ads.
